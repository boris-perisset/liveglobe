Live Globe

News where it happens

About this project

Live Globe shows where news comes from — and how far it travels. Rather than being another news aggregator, it makes the movement of attention through the media landscape visible: who reports, who stays silent, and when a story crosses a border or a language.

This is a prompt for thought, not a research instrument. Assigning reports to places, topics and events remains an estimate — which is why every marker leads to the original source, so you can look for yourself. Article contents are not stored, only metadata and links.

Where the idea comes from

The starting point is radio.garden — a globe with radio stations standing where they broadcast. I cite it regularly in my UX/UI teaching as an example of how much a representation can achieve beyond listing the data. The same stations in a table would be a directory. On the globe they become an experience of nearness and distance: you turn the sphere and hear how far away the world sounds.

Live Globe carries that idea over to the news. Radio stations become media outlets, the broadcast area becomes the place where something happened. The question behind it stays the same — what does a map show that a list cannot?

This site would not exist without these projects

Everything here rests on work that others made freely available — often over years and mostly unpaid. Warm thanks to everyone involved.

Data

  • The GDELT Project Reports, places and topics · free for non-commercial use
  • OpenStreetMap Map data · © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0
  • Natural Earth Country borders · public domain
  • IPTC Media Topics Topic taxonomy · CC BY 4.0
  • Media Cloud Register of media outlets — country, region, language and publishing frequency. An academic undertaking with open access; the API client is Apache 2.0.
  • Wikidata Editorial addresses of media outlets · CC0 1.0
  • Nominatim Place search when locating newsrooms · © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0
  • GeoNames Place resolution · CC BY 4.0

Map

  • MapLibre GL JS Globe and map rendering · BSD-3-Clause
  • OpenFreeMap Vector tiles, free and without a key · donated by Zsolt Ero
  • OpenMapTiles Tile schema and base style · BSD-3-Clause

Tools

  • PostgreSQL · PostGIS Storage and geo queries · PostgreSQL licence, GPL-2.0-or-later
  • Supabase Database, API and scheduling · Apache-2.0
  • Deno Runtime of the data fetcher · MIT
  • Vite · TypeScript Build and language of the frontend · MIT, Apache-2.0

Typeface

  • Nohemi Designed by Rajesh Rajput and released as freeware — free to use for private and commercial projects alike. The font files themselves may not be modified; every right beyond use stays with the designer. It carries the entire look of this site, and for that he deserves his own thanks.
